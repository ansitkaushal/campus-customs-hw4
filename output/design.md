# Design: The Varsity Locker Room

The site is now themed as a game-day campus store, "The Varsity Locker Room."

- **Fonts and color:** Varsity block lettering (Graduate) for headings and clean Inter for everything else. The colors are navy and cream with gold accents, and the main buy buttons ("Shop the collection" and "Add to bag") are solid gold. Red appears only on low-stock and sold-out badges. Customers recognize "school merch" right away, and their eye goes straight to the gold buttons.
- **Hierarchy:** Each page has one clear focal point. The hero says "Wear your Yale pride." with a single gold call-to-action button, followed by a scoreboard of store stats, category patches and big, bold prices. With fewer competing elements, customers decide faster and fewer leave.
- **Product cards:** Cards look like tilted hang tags, with a punched hole and string. On hover they straighten and lift, and some carry badges like "Fan Favorite," "New Drop" or "Only 3 sizes left." Each card also shows a row of size chips, with sold-out sizes crossed out and sizes that are running low in red. The interaction invites browsing, and the badges add social proof and urgency.
- **Motion:** A promo ticker runs across the top, products fade up as you scroll, and the chat panel slides in when opened. Adding an item to the bag triggers a confetti burst. All of this switches off for shoppers who have reduced motion turned on. Small rewards keep people scrolling and make buying feel good.
- **Chat:** "Benny," a friendly store clerk with a bulldog avatar, chats in text-message style with a typing indicator and live status lines like "Checking stock…". Quick-reply chips get the conversation started, and product cards appear inside the chat. Each card opens the product page, where the shopper picks a size and adds it to the bag. It feels like talking to a person, which builds trust, and customers are one click from the item they asked about.

Overall, the hover effects, animations and chat give people reasons to stay, and the consistent gold buy buttons, urgency badges and simple pick-a-size, add-to-bag flow make it easier to buy.
